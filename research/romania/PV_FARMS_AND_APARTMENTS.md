# Integrating Romanian PV farms and apartment buildings

Research checked on **5 October 2026**. The proposed pilot is in Bucharest/Ilfov, using the public electricity network and the dynamic-price approach already studied for GridLink.

## Finding and recommended route

The commercial opportunity exists: Romanian solar projects can sell on market-linked terms without a long-term fixed-price power purchase agreement (PPA). A licensed supplier can buy an agreed portion of their output and supply a group of consenting apartment customers. GridLink can provide consumption forecasts, producer matching, contract comparisons and savings reporting.

My recommendation is to start with **one distribution-connected PV producer, one licensed supplier and one building with separately enrolled apartments**. Use ordinary supplier contracts for the first commercial pilot. Investigate a registered energy community alongside that pilot, with its membership, asset rights and settlement arrangements confirmed before using community-sharing benefits in customer offers.

The main qualifications are:

- A producer selling electricity has market-access, sale and balancing arrangements. Having no fixed-price PPA does not mean having no contracts at all.
- A commercial solar-farm company cannot automatically become a renewable energy community member. Its principal business matters.
- Community registration and the adoption of wholesale/storage rules do not prove that the distribution operator can already allocate energy to apartment bills.
- Solar generation and apartment demand must be compared in the same settlement intervals. Annual production exceeding annual consumption does not establish full coverage.
- A battery is optional for the initial arrangement. It can improve the match between midday production and evening demand, subject to its cost and the applicable settlement model.

The legal assessment below identifies feasible routes and points requiring confirmation for a specific project. Before accepting members or signing energy-sale contracts, have a Romanian energy-law adviser and the proposed licensed supplier review the actual structure, and obtain the distribution operator's written confirmation of its metering and settlement arrangements.

## 2. What “selling at a low buying price” means

A merchant producer's relevant alternative is its **net realised revenue**, weighted by actual production. It may sell through a trader, supplier or aggregator on a price linked to PZU, with management and imbalance costs deducted. The price during solar production can be considerably below the daily market average.

Our existing OPCOM analysis found wholesale averages for 10:00–17:00 of approximately **0.154 RON/kWh in April, 0.258 in July and 0.448 in 1–28 September 2026**. These are averages over selected periods, excluding network charges and taxes, and are not producer purchase offers. They support investigating low solar capture prices; they do not establish what a particular farm receives. See [the collected Romanian price analysis](REPORT.md) and [OPCOM's original results](https://www.opcom.ro/grafice-ip-raportPIP-si-volumTranzactionat/ro).

A utility-scale producer should not be treated as a household prosumer receiving a universal regulated buyback price. The commercial comparison should use the actual seller's contract and settlement statements. Also, OUG 163/2022 Article 22^1 separates community membership from the Article 73^1 prosumer regime: existing prosumer benefits cannot simply be added to community benefits in the financial model. [Current OUG 163/2022](https://legislatie.just.ro/Public/DetaliiDocument/307059).

For the user-requested dynamic tariff, compare alternatives interval by interval. A fixed solar purchase price can exceed the customer's existing dynamic energy price on a sunny, cheap day. Paying the producer more while charging the consumer less requires a positive margin after all operating costs, or an explicitly priced hedge against other price movements.

## 4. Can a commercial PV farm join the community?

For a **renewable energy community, CESR**, OUG 163/2022 Article 22(1) limits private-enterprise participation where electricity production is the enterprise's principal commercial/professional activity. A company whose business is running a solar farm therefore faces a substantive membership obstacle. A business with incidental PV, such as a factory producing primarily for its own needs, requires a different assessment. CESR also requires eligible membership, local control and generation/consumption connections under the same distribution operator. [OUG 163/2022, Articles 2(18) and 22](https://legislatie.just.ro/Public/DetaliiDocument/307059).

For a **citizens' energy community, CEC**, Law 123/2012 Article 3(24), amended by OUG 59/2025 Article IV, requires voluntary participation, effective control by at least five eligible natural-person/local-authority/small-enterprise members or shareholders, community benefits and consumption connections under the same distributor. This different definition warrants investigation for a producer partnership, but does not establish that a large energy company can control the entity or that its plant qualifies for the proposed sharing arrangement. [Official Senate copy of OUG 59/2025, Article IV, PDF page 109](https://www.senat.ro/legis/PDF/2025/25L467OG.PDF?nocache=true).

A separate external PPA does not itself make the producer a member or turn purchased electricity into output from a community-owned plant. For genuine community generation, investigate ownership, an eligible producer member or another legally recognised asset arrangement. A lease or asset-management agreement would need confirmation of the community's rights and regulatory treatment; it is not an automatic substitute for ownership.

The robust commercial fallback is to keep the farm as an **external seller** and deliver its electricity through a licensed supplier. This avoids depending on the farm's admission as a CESR member.

## 5. Implementation options

| Option | Producer's position | Apartment relationship | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Supplier purchases PV output and supplies the customer group | External seller under a physical PPA/offtake contract | Each customer signs the licensed supplier's retail contract | Recommended first commercial pilot; ordinary supply route. |
| B. Registered CEC/CESR sharing | Community-owned generation or a legally eligible member/asset arrangement | Community participation plus supply/allocation and residual-supply arrangements | Good longer-term route; confirm governance, asset eligibility and final settlement capability. |
| C. Building rooftop PV serving common areas | Building/association owns or appropriately contracts the installation | Common-area electricity account receives physical self-consumption benefits | Useful small pilot; individual apartment savings need a separate arrangement. |
| D. Financial/virtual PPA | Farm and buyer settle a price difference financially | Apartments retain normal electricity supply contracts | Does not itself deliver allocated energy or remove network charges; poor first household pilot. |

### Option A: supplier-mediated delivery

The physical electricity uses the public grid. Contractually:

```text
PV producer -- agreed output and price --> licensed supplier / balancing partner
licensed supplier -- electricity supply and bills --> participating apartments
GridLink -- forecasts, comparisons and reporting --> producer, supplier and members
```

For a first contract, negotiate payment for the actual agreed PV output rather than a rigid requirement to deliver solar electricity every evening. The supplier buys any shortfall and handles surplus according to the agreement. This avoids placing an unpriced production/consumption mismatch obligation on the producer or on GridLink.

The agreement should explicitly state the energy volume committed, whether it covers all or part of generation, the price formula, negative-price treatment, production curtailment, forecasting and imbalance costs, guarantee requirements, credit risk, surplus sale, duration, exit rights and who bears shortfall costs. Producer and supplier fees should be visible in the savings calculation.

A single supplier is the simplest pilot arrangement. It can combine the agreed solar component with residual energy in its offered retail product. GridLink's indicative allocations must reconcile to the supplier's recognised settlement, rather than becoming independent utility invoices. A grouped procurement product can operate without asserting community-specific tariff benefits. This recommended structure is an implementation assessment based on the existing licensed supply route; the actual product requires a supplier offer.

### Option B: formal community sharing

OUG 163/2022 Article 22^5 permits licensing or delegation to a third-party supplier/aggregator. Its exceptions include specified nonprofit forms, certain isolated direct-line arrangements and condominiums whose own production is entirely intended for self-consumption. An exemption from a supply/aggregation licence does not remove other market, metering or consumer obligations. [ANRE's explanation of the statutory licensing framework](https://anre.ro/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Referat-de-aprobare.pdf).

Use the adopted registration procedure to prepare governance, membership/POD records, asset information and smart-meter integration. It distinguishes CEC's same-distributor consumption condition from CESR's same-distributor consumption-and-production condition, and establishes a 180-day conformity period. Registration alone cannot validate our savings assumptions. [Order 50 procedure, Articles 4–7](https://legislatie.just.ro/Public/DetaliiDocument/311918).

For Bucharest/Ilfov, the concession distributor is **Rețele Electrice România**. Verify the operator on each actual connection certificate: a plant's county does not establish whether it connects to that distributor, another network or directly to Transelectrica. A large transmission-connected park should not be assumed eligible for a distribution-level sharing arrangement. [ANRE's distributor directory](https://anre.ro/consumatori/afla-care-este-distribuitorul-tau-de-energie-electrica/).

The October proposal envisages community/member contracts, residual or single-supplier contracts and a multipartite agreement with the distributor and residual suppliers. Distributor-validated interval allocation would use proportional consumption or fixed percentages, capped by concurrent consumption. Community invoices would cover allocated energy; residual-supplier invoices would cover residual energy and applicable network charges on measured consumption. These remain proposed operational details. [Draft Articles 46–49](https://anre.ro/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Proiect-de-ordin-pentru-modificarea-Regulamentului-de-furnizare.docx).

Before proceeding, obtain the supplier's and distributor's written answers on supported meter intervals, data corrections, invoice responsibility, entry/exit processes and the start date of recognised sharing. A commercial supplier-mediated pilot can proceed on its own agreed terms while those questions are resolved.

### Option C: start with the building's common-area meter

Rooftop PV physically offsetting lifts, pumps and lighting can provide a clear first measurement boundary. Ownership/roof-use consent, connection approval, installation design and the applicable prosumer/community arrangement still need to be resolved.

The common-area meter and residents' individual meters represent different electricity accounts. Savings on the association's account can reduce common expenses; they cannot be presented as a measured reduction in every apartment's private electricity bill.

An adjacent direct line can be assessed separately where land rights, electrical design and approvals make it practical. Do not assume that a distant field and a residential block can become a private network merely by signing an agreement. Law 123's closed-distribution-system provisions generally concern industrial/commercial/service sites and exclude ordinary household supply, with limited exceptions. [Law 123/2012, Article 50](https://legislatie.just.ro/Public/DetaliiDocument/139677).

## 6. Exactly how apartments would be enrolled and accounted for

The following is a proposed pilot workflow. Its community-sharing variant must be updated to the final ANRE supply rules and the distributor's accepted process.

1. **Collect the electricity account structure.** Identify the common-area POD and each participating apartment's POD, account holder, supplier, meter type, tariff, connection capacity and authorised access to readings. Owners and tenants may differ from electricity contract holders.
2. **Obtain individual participation.** Record consent from the account holder for data access and the required energy contracts. The association can coordinate recruitment; its own enrollment does not automatically switch or bind residents' separate accounts. Preserve withdrawal and supplier-choice rights.
3. **Collect comparable data.** Obtain at least an initial several weeks of aligned production and consumption intervals, then extend across seasons. Use existing history where available. A full year is preferable for sizing and commercial commitments. Monthly totals are insufficient for simultaneous energy matching.
4. **Obtain three comparable offers.** Compare the current customer tariff, the producer-backed supplier product and a formal-sharing proposal where operationally available. Include every fee, tax, imbalance assumption and contract obligation.
5. **Agree allocation and residual supply.** Decide how participation shares are set and how unused allocations are redistributed. Each interval must conserve energy and leave unmet apartment demand assigned to its recognised supplier.
6. **Run advisory accounting.** Replay validated meter intervals alongside existing bills. Report predicted and realised savings separately; do not replace utility bills during this phase.
7. **Start recognised settlement.** Begin live billing only under signed contracts and the accepted supplier/distributor arrangement. Store original readings, corrections, approved allocation versions, contract versions and invoice references.
8. **Expand using measured demand.** Admit new consumers after calculating their effect on simultaneous coverage, supplier exposure and connection limits. Add daytime businesses or controllable EV charging where they complement the residential profile.

An illustrative interval shows the accounting:

```text
Available agreed PV output:                100 kWh
Participating apartment demand:             70 kWh
Maximum concurrent PV coverage:             70 kWh
Unmatched PV output:                        30 kWh
```

Without storage or a different supplier procurement/hedging agreement, those 30 kWh cannot be assigned as physical solar coverage of evening apartment demand. If the interval instead has 120 kWh demand, 100 kWh can be covered and 20 kWh remains residual supply.

For a proportional illustration, an apartment consuming 3 kWh in an interval with 90 kWh total demand and 60 kWh available PV receives 2 kWh of PV coverage and 1 kWh of residual energy. A fixed-share approach needs a rule for an apartment whose share exceeds its actual interval consumption. The final operational rule must be the one recognised by the parties.

## 7. Where the savings would come from

Evaluate both sides against their actual alternatives:

```text
Producer improvement = new net producer revenue - existing net producer revenue

Customer saving = existing full electricity bill - new full electricity bill

Available margin = avoided supplier energy cost
                 - producer purchase price
                 - balancing, administration, credit and platform costs
```

The transport/distribution split already adjustable in GridLink is an internal commercial allocation. A 50/50 split does not change the regulated network charge or its legally designated payer. Any producer reimbursement must appear in the contracts and in the correct ledger. Community-wide savings must exclude transfers between members.

For the initial business case, keep applicable network charges and taxes in the bill. Assess any community-specific distribution treatment separately using the applicable ANRE methodology and the actual approved tariff. Do not budget an automatic exemption from transmission, distribution, VAT or other contributions.

### Arithmetic example, with assumed prices

These values illustrate the calculation; they are **not Romanian supplier quotes or measured GridLink results**. All prices exclude VAT; the common charge component is an assumption held equal between alternatives.

| Component | Current supply | PV-backed covered energy |
|---|---:|---:|
| Energy component | 0.65 RON/kWh | 0.35 RON/kWh paid to producer |
| Additional partner/platform cost | Included in baseline above | 0.05 RON/kWh |
| Other applicable charges, assumed unchanged | 0.40 RON/kWh | 0.40 RON/kWh |
| Total | **1.05 RON/kWh** | **0.80 RON/kWh** |

The saving is 0.25 RON per covered kWh, or **23.8% on that portion**. If PV covers only 30% of monthly demand and the remaining tariff is unchanged, the total bill reduction is **7.1%**, before additional fixed fees. For an assumed 15,000 kWh monthly building demand, that is 4,500 covered kWh and 1,125 RON monthly savings excluding VAT. If the producer's existing net receipt were 0.20 RON/kWh, receiving 0.35 would improve its receipt by 75% on contracted output. That existing receipt is also an assumption.

For a dynamic contract, replace the assumed 0.65 with the actual interval-dependent baseline. The same solar purchase price can generate savings in one interval and a loss in another. Evaluate the complete seasonal profile before offering a percentage discount.

## 8. Battery, EV and weather strategy

The first question is how much contracted PV can serve concurrent customer demand without storage. Compare extra consumers and directly scheduled EV charging before investing in a stationary battery. An EV plugged in at noon can absorb low-price solar directly; an EV arriving only at night has a different opportunity.

A battery at the PV field can shift export timing. It does not physically offset an apartment's import behind that apartment's meter. Model remote delivery fees, allocation rights, stored-energy origin, conversion losses, degradation, contracted volumes and export limits before importing the existing battery ROI estimates into this arrangement.

For a registered community, the statutory priorities favour member consumption and charging/discharging arrangements that serve the community; external trading concerns surplus after member needs. A live optimiser therefore needs constraints implementing those priorities. It should not automatically export energy for a high price while leaving eligible simultaneous member demand unsupplied by available community generation. [OUG 163/2022, Article 22^5](https://legislatie.just.ro/Public/DetaliiDocument/307059).

Weather can improve production and residual-demand estimates. Local sunshine combined with weak national wind/solar can make uncommitted surplus more valuable, while widespread sunshine can depress its price. Use published day-ahead prices when available and forecasts for uncertain volumes or later periods. Balance any expected revenue against errors and the supplier's settlement costs. The current regional indicators do not establish a measured national shortage.

The research battery returns are conditional simulations under their stated meter/contract assumptions. They are not an ROI result for a remote PV field supplying a residential block.

## 9. Prospect selection and questions for later outreach

The best first prospect is likely an operating, distribution-connected producer with a short or renewable market-linked sale arrangement, reliable interval readings and willingness to commit a modest share of output. The size should follow recruited customer demand. A very large farm may have complex financing obligations and a connection topology unsuitable for community sharing.

For each producer, request:

- Legal entity, ownership, main activity, plant location, installed/export capacity and commissioning status.
- Connection certificate and actual network operator; production/export readings with timestamps and units.
- Current offtake/management agreement, term, termination costs, exclusivity and uncommitted output.
- Actual net receipts and imbalance/management deductions across several seasons.
- Existing PPAs, CfD/support obligations, financing covenants and consent requirements.
- Price/volume offer, minimum guaranteed revenue, negative-price policy and curtailment responsibility.
- Willingness to be an external seller, eligible member or asset partner, with each structure assessed separately.

For the supplier, request a household-compatible product, clear producer procurement and residual-price formulas, balancing fees, credit/security terms, recognised meter intervals, billing responsibility and support for the intended community arrangement.

For the building, establish willing account holders, consent for readings, meter coverage, tariff histories, daily profiles, common-area use and EV availability. Test customer retention and withdrawal assumptions before making a long-duration volume commitment to a producer.

No producer, supplier, resident or regulator was contacted as part of this research.

## 10. Consequences for the current GridLink prototype

The current code provides demonstration participant allocations and a battery replay under a shared-meter assumption. It does not implement licensed electricity supply, producer onboarding/legal checks, individual utility POD settlement, a distributor connection, community registration or enforceable utility invoices.

A small next software step would be an **advisory PV-to-building comparison** using imported interval data. Extend the existing replay inputs and accounting rather than creating another optimisation engine. It should record each POD's distributor and contract, the producer's committed output, alternative procurement prices, recognised allocation rule, residual energy, external fees and the source of stored energy. Keep indicative results separate from approved supplier/distributor settlement.

The minimum comparison should report simultaneous PV coverage, whole-bill savings for each apartment, the producer's net revenue change, surplus and residual volumes, partner costs and community-wide net benefit. It should compare current supply, supplier-mediated PV supply and a legally validated sharing scenario, each with and without storage. Do not reuse the current illustrative transport split as a regulated tariff reduction.

The commercial pilot can be prepared now through a licensed supplier. Live community-sharing bills should follow a confirmed structure, the applicable final rules and functioning settlement arrangements.
